# Customers can import tasks from a CSV file.

A customer hands the app their own task spreadsheet and sees the whole list appear. Priya has forty tasks in a CSV from her team's tracker; today she retypes each one, after this she uploads the file and works from it.

**For example.** Priya keeps her team's tasks in a spreadsheet — a CSV her project tracker produces, forty rows, one per task. She wants to work from them in the app, where she can tick them off. Today the app takes one task at a time, typed in by hand, so she opens the spreadsheet and retypes all forty — about fifteen minutes, and she gives up partway and keeps two lists. After this, she saves the file, hands it to the app, and the forty tasks appear in her list, ready to work on. If a row is missing a title she is told which line is the problem, in words, rather than being handed a half-imported list or a silent failure.

**Who it's for, and when.** For anyone who already keeps their tasks somewhere else — a spreadsheet, another tracker's export — and is deciding whether to move them here. *When I already have my tasks in a spreadsheet, I want to bring the whole list into the app at once, so I can start working from it instead of typing every task in by hand.*

**Today.** The app only knows tasks you type into it, one at a time. Anyone arriving with a list — forty rows in a spreadsheet, an export from another tool — has to retype it, or keep two lists and reconcile them by eye. Priya retyped her forty tasks once already and has been maintaining both copies since, because there is no way to hand the app a file.

**Why now.** A spreadsheet is where most people's tasks already live before they come here, and taking a file is the smallest honest counterpart to the export we just shipped: a customer can get their data out, and now should be able to bring it in. The earlier we learn how people's files are actually shaped, the sooner we find out which rows we get wrong.

**What changes for them.** One file in, one list out. A customer hands the app a CSV of their tasks and sees them listed, ready to work on — one task per row. A row without a title is reported with the line it came from, in words a person can read, rather than silently dropped or half-imported. An empty file, or one with only the column names, leaves their list unchanged rather than failing. And the tasks land in their own list, not somebody else's.

**How we'll know.** People who arrive with an existing list import it rather than retyping, and their first list is populated within minutes of signing up. Files come from more than one source — a spreadsheet, another tool's export — rather than only the one we designed against. And nobody writes in to ask us to add their tasks for them.

**Not doing.** No bringing tasks *out* — that is the export program's job. No format other than CSV. No scheduled or watched imports. No mapping screen to match their columns to ours — the file is taken as it is. No updating or removing existing tasks from a file, only adding the ones it names. And no surviving a restart of the app, since the list does not yet.
